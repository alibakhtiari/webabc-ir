Determining how much a website will cost depends on complexity, features, and platform choices, and the honest answer to "how much does a website cost" is always "it depends" — but the factors it depends on are predictable and worth understanding before you request a quote.

## Factors Driving Costs

### 1. Platform Choice

- **WordPress**: Economical and quick setup for standard business portfolios, blogs, and stores with typical e-commerce needs. Lower upfront cost, but ongoing plugin and theme licensing, plus more frequent maintenance, over the site's lifetime.
- **Custom Coding (Next.js/Astro)**: A higher upfront investment in exchange for a faster, more secure, and more precisely-built application with no dependency on third-party plugins that can break with an update. This usually pays off for sites with real scale, unique functionality, or performance requirements that a CMS architecture can't meet.

Neither option is objectively "better" — the right one depends on your catalog size, content update frequency, and how custom your functional requirements actually are.

### 2. Page Count

Every unique page layout adds design mockup hours and coding hours, on top of content writing if you need that too. A 5-page brochure site and a 40-page site with unique templates for services, locations, and case studies are fundamentally different projects, even on the same platform.

### 3. Special Features

Add-ons like multi-language support (with real RTL/LTR layout work, not just translated text), custom database-backed features, secure member portals, booking systems, or payment integrations each scale development time independently of page count. A simple 10-page site with a complex booking system can cost more than a 30-page site with none of that.

### 4. Design Depth

A template-based design with light customization costs far less than fully custom UI/UX work, wireframing, and design iteration. The tradeoff is brand differentiation: heavily templated sites tend to look like every other site built on the same theme.

## Costs Quotes Often Leave Out

Many quotes cover the build but not what comes after. Budget separately for hosting (which varies significantly based on traffic and platform), a domain and SSL certificate, ongoing software updates, security monitoring, and periodic content refreshes. A site that launches perfectly and is then never updated becomes a security and performance liability within a year, regardless of how well it was built initially.

## Why the Cheapest Quote Isn't Always the Cheapest Outcome

A low quote that skips proper technical foundations, security hardening, or scalability planning often costs more in total once you factor in an early rebuild, lost search rankings from technical debt, or a security incident. Compare quotes on scope and what's included long-term, not just the headline number.
