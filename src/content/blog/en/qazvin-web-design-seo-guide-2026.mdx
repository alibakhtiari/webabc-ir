## 4. Local Content and Geo-Landing Strategy for Qazvin

Local content wins in Qazvin when it mirrors how buyers phrase their searches: service plus city, service plus industrial zone, and specialty plus neighbourhood, each answered on a page carrying the address, phone, and service area the searcher is verifying. City-generic copy ranks for nothing, while pages written for one procurement manager's query capture the traffic that converts.

Build the structure in four moves:

- **Map the real query patterns.** Industrial buyers append zone names ("Caspian Industrial City"), patients append street or district names, and retail visitors append "Qazvin" to brand-neutral categories. Group keywords by those three intents before writing anything.
- **Give every intent one page.** One service area, one primary query, one page — with the NAP block, embedded map, sector-specific proof, and internal links from the relevant blog posts. Splitting one intent across three near-identical pages makes all three rank worse.
- **Publish proof, not slogans.** Project galleries, export documentation notes, equipment specification summaries, and practitioner credentials are the content that earns links and reviews from Qazvin buyers; generic service descriptions earn neither.
- **Feed the geo pages from the blog.** Each new article should link to the service or area page it supports, so topical relevance flows to the pages that actually convert inquiries into contracts.

Review acquisition closes the loop: ask completed buyers and patients for reviews through a consistent path, respond to every one in the local language, and let that volume reinforce the profile built in section 3.
