**Direct answer:** How much does a website cost in 2026? Expect $800–$2,000 for a landing page, $2,000–$5,000 for a professional business site, $3,500–$12,000 for ecommerce, and $10,000+ for custom web apps. Your final website development cost depends on site type, design depth, and integrations.

If you have searched "how much does a website cost in 2026" and found quotes from $500 to $50,000, you are not alone. "A website" is not one product — a five-page brochure site and a multilingual ecommerce platform share almost nothing except the name.

## How Much Does Web Development Cost by Website Type in 2026?

Start here. Find your site type in this 2026 price-band table, then adjust up or down using the factors in the next section.

| Website type in 2026 | Typical website development cost | Best for | Timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page / brochure (1–5 pages) | $800–$2,000 | Startups, local services, campaigns | 1–3 weeks |
| Professional business site (5–15 pages) | $2,000–$5,000 | B2B, agencies, clinics, contractors | 3–5 weeks |
| Corporate / content-heavy site (20+ pages, blog, 2 languages) | $4,000–$9,000 | Mid-size firms, media, regional brands | 4–8 weeks |
| Standard ecommerce store | $3,500–$8,000 | Retailers, wholesalers, DTC brands | 4–6 weeks |
| Custom ecommerce / headless commerce | $8,000–$20,000+ | Large catalogs, ERP/CRM sync, multi-currency | 6–12 weeks |
| Custom web app / portal / SaaS MVP | $10,000–$25,000+ | Bookings, dashboards, memberships | 8–14 weeks |

These are market-rate bands for professional work — design, build, responsive QA, basic on-page SEO, and launch — not DIY builders or the cheapest offshore template installs. Ongoing hosting, maintenance, content, and marketing are extra, typically 10–20% of build cost per year for small sites.

Why such wide bands? Because three identical-looking quotes can hide very different scopes. One includes custom design, speed optimization, and schema; another reuses a heavy theme with no performance work. Always compare itemized scope, not just the headline price.

## What Factors Drive Website Development Cost the Most?

If two agencies quote $2,000 and $7,000 for the "same" site, the difference is almost always one of these six factors:

**1. Page count and unique layouts.** A 5-page site with one template is fast. A 30-page site with distinct layouts for services, locations, case studies, and blog needs far more design and build hours — plus content entry.

**2. Design depth.** Template customization ($1,000–$2,500 range) means adapting pre-built sections. Custom UI/UX ($3,500–$10,000+ as a standalone phase) means wireframes, Figma prototypes, a reusable design system, and motion details. Custom looks unique and converts better, but costs more upfront.

**3. Content and copywriting.** Many quotes exclude copy. Professional English copy, plus Persian or Arabic localization with true RTL layout work, adds real hours. Stock photos are cheap; art-directed photography and product imagery are not.

**4. Integrations and functionality.** Payments, booking, membership login, CRM or ERP sync, SMS APIs, shipment tracking, and multi-vendor logic each add engineering time independent of page count. A 10-page site with complex booking can cost more than a 30-page brochure site.

**5. Multilingual and regional needs.** Real bilingual builds need hreflang, separate sitemaps, RTL/LTR QA, translated slugs, and localized schema — not just a translate plugin. Budget 20–40% extra for a properly engineered second language.

**6. SEO and performance foundation.** Basic meta tags are table stakes. Core Web Vitals tuning, image pipelines, caching and CDN setup, Schema.org markup, and search architecture take extra hours but decide whether the site ranks and converts. Skipping this is the most common cause of a "cheap site, then expensive rebuild" cycle.

Rule of thumb: every custom layout, integration, and language multiplies QA too. That is why itemized estimates beat flat "business site: $X" menus.

## How Much Does WordPress Cost vs Custom Development in 2026?

This is the most common follow-up to "how much does web development cost," and the honest answer is: WordPress is cheaper to start, custom is often cheaper to own.

|  | WordPress / WooCommerce | Custom (Astro / Next.js / headless) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparable business site, upfront | $900–$3,500 | $2,500–$8,000+ |
| Comparable standard store, upfront | $1,500–$4,000 | $4,500–$12,000+ |
| Speed out of the box | Depends on theme/plugins | Fast by default, less bloat |
| Security surface | Larger (core + themes + plugins) | Smaller (no plugin stack to patch) |
| 2–3 year upkeep | Plugin/theme licenses + patching + fixes | Mostly hosting + minor updates |
| Best when | Standard blog, brochure, simple store, frequent DIY edits | Performance, security, scale, custom UX, headless commerce |

Choose WordPress when your needs are standard — a brochure site, blog, or straightforward store — and your team wants to edit content themselves without developer help. A lean setup with a reputable theme and minimal plugins is the value sweet spot.

Choose custom when speed, security, or uniqueness drive revenue: high-traffic lead gen, content sites competing on Core Web Vitals, stores with custom checkout or ERP needs, bilingual RTL/LTR sites, or anything that must not break when a plugin updates. Higher upfront website development cost buys lower maintenance drag and fewer rebuilds.

The trap to avoid: a $900 WordPress build loaded with 30 plugins that needs $150/month in licenses, emergency fixes, and a full rebuild in 18 months. Total cost of ownership matters more than day-one price. If you go WordPress, pay for restraint — fewer plugins, managed hosting, and update care.

## How Much Does Custom Ecommerce Website Development Cost in 2026?

Ecommerce has the widest variance, because "store" can mean 20 products with card payments or 20,000 SKUs with multi-warehouse sync. Here is how custom ecommerce website development cost breaks down in 2026:

**Standard store: $3,500–$8,000.** Catalog setup, card and local payment gateways, shipping rules, coupons, transactional emails, and basic SEO. Usually WooCommerce or Shopify with tasteful customization. Fits most single-country DTC and wholesale starters.

**Advanced / headless store: $8,000–$20,000+.** Headless storefront (Next.js/Astro + Shopify, Medusa, or custom Node), custom checkout, multi-currency and multi-language, ERP/CRM/inventory sync, loyalty or subscriptions, and performance engineering for large catalogs. Fits regional sellers, marketplaces, and brands where checkout speed directly moves revenue.

What pushes you from the first band to the second?

- Catalog size and variants (100 vs 10,000 SKUs, bundles, configurable products)
- Checkout complexity (guest + login + BNPL + local gateways + fraud rules)
- Integrations (accounting, warehouse, CRM, SMS, shipment tracking, POS)
- Languages and currencies (RTL support, hreflang, localized pricing and tax)
- Migration (redirect maps, order history, SEO preservation from an old store)

If an agency quotes ecommerce without asking about SKUs, payments, shipping zones, and returns flow, the quote is a guess. Get those four answers in writing before you compare numbers. Our [ecommerce services](/en/services/ecommerce/) checklist covers the scope items worth itemizing.

## How Can You Budget Without Overpaying? A 7-Step Checklist

Use this numbered checklist before you request quotes. It takes 30 minutes and typically saves one full revision round:

1. **Define the site type from the table above.** Pick one row. If you sit between two rows, budget the higher one — scope almost always grows.
2. **List pages and unique layouts, not just pages.** Homepage, service detail, location page, blog index, and checkout each count separately for design.
3. **Decide WordPress vs custom using the table above.** Standard + DIY edits lean WordPress; speed, scale, or custom UX leans custom.
4. **Itemize integrations.** Payments, booking, login, CRM, ERP, SMS, and multilingual — each on its own line with must-have vs nice-to-have.
5. **Separate content costs.** Who writes copy? Who provides product data and images? Unassigned content is the number one launch delayer.
6. **Ask for total cost of ownership.** Build price plus year-one hosting, licenses, maintenance, and content updates. Compare 2-year totals, not day-one totals.
7. **Get an instant band first, then an itemized quote.** Run the [Website Cost Calculator](/en/tools/cost-calculator/) for a market-rate band, then ask agencies to quote against your checklist line by line.

Bring this checklist to any [web development](/en/services/web-development/) discovery call. Agencies quote faster and more accurately when scope arrives structured — and vague quotes become easy to spot.

## Bottom Line

For 2026 budgeting, anchor on site type: $2,000–$5,000 covers most professional business sites, $3,500–$8,000 covers standard ecommerce, and anything headless, multilingual, or deeply integrated starts near $8,000 and scales with complexity. Web design costs 2026 reward restraint — fewer templates, fewer plugins, faster foundations — more than they reward the lowest bid.
