Search engines in 2026 reward sites that load lightning fast, deliver frictionless user experiences, and prove authentic subject-matter authority. While the fundamentals of crawlability, relevance, and authority remain essential, the bar has risen significantly. Google now evaluates interactive responsiveness via INP, answers complex queries directly through AI Overviews, and prioritizes proven first-hand experience. In this guide, we walk through the exact best practices and thresholds we implement across client sites at WebABC.

## 1. Core Web Vitals: Hit the New Thresholds

Core Web Vitals pass when the main content paints within 2.5 seconds, interactions respond within 200 milliseconds, and layout shift stays under 0.1 — Google's thresholds for page experience, measured at the 75th percentile of visits. A failing page still competes, but with a handicap against equivalent pages that pass, so page experience is table stakes, not a tiebreaker.

- **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)**: your main content should render in under 2.5 seconds on a mid-tier phone over 4G. Oversized hero images and render-blocking scripts are the usual culprits.
- **Interaction to Next Paint (INP)** has fully replaced First Input Delay. Where FID only measured the first tap, INP scores every interaction across the whole visit. Target under 200 milliseconds at the 75th percentile.
- **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)**: keep it under 0.1. Reserve space for images, ads, and embeds so content doesn't jump as the page loads.

Start by testing templates, not individual pages — if your blog post template is slow, every post is slow. Compress and serve modern image formats, preload your hero asset, defer third-party scripts, and self-host fonts where possible. Read field data over lab scores: a page can pass a synthetic test and still fail on real phones, which is where the ranking signal comes from. ## 2. E-E-A-T and AI Overviews: Prove You Actually Know This

E-E-A-T — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness — separates pages written by someone who has done the work from pages generated about it, and in 2026 it doubles as the filter for AI Overview citations: thin, generic content doesn't get cited, it gets bypassed. The framework isn't a formula you type in; it's signals readers and crawlers can verify.

Practical ways to build E-E-A-T into your content:

- **Show first-hand experience**: original screenshots, real project examples, and "here's what happened when we tried this" detail that AI-generated filler can't fake.
- **Name real authors**: bylines with credentials, linked author pages, and consistent publishing history.
- **Cite primary sources**: link out to official documentation and original research rather than other blogs summarizing it.
- **Cover topics completely**: a cluster of interconnected articles on one subject signals topical authority better than one long page and ten orphans.
- **Show the entity behind the site**: a real address, real contact routes, working policy pages, and consistent naming across the web — trust signals a machine can cross-check.

Keep your prose readable while you do it — short sentences and clear structure help both users and machine summarizers. Our [readability checker](/en/tools/readability-checker/) gives you a quick grade before you publish.

## 3. Mobile-First Indexing Still Sets the Floor

Mobile-first indexing means Google evaluates the mobile version of your site as the real version — content, internal links, and structured data that exist only on desktop effectively do not exist for ranking. If a section renders desktop-only, it is unranked, and no amount of desktop polish compensates. The checklist below keeps both renderings equal so nothing is silently excluded.

The checklist:

- Content parity: same text, headings, and schema on mobile as desktop.
- Tap targets sized for thumbs, with adequate spacing.
- Images optimized for mobile bandwidth — a 2MB hero that looks fine on office Wi-Fi destroys LCP on a phone connection.
- Test on real devices, not just the browser emulator. Cheap Android phones on congested networks are where your customers actually are.
- Interstitials and pop-ups that obscure the main content on load — Google indexes what the visitor sees first, so a wall over the page costs both users and visibility.

## 4. Technical Hygiene: Boring, Unsexy, Still Decisive

Technical hygiene is the set of unglamorous fixes — valid structured data, working internal links, correct canonicals and hreflang, a clean crawl surface — that rarely wins new rankings on its own but quietly caps everything else when broken. It's decisive because failures are invisible: the site looks fine while pages are misconsolidated, unfetched, or stripped of markup.

- **Structured data**: Organization, Article, FAQ, LocalBusiness, and Product schema help search engines parse your pages and unlock rich results. Validate after every template change.
- **Internal linking**: every important page should be reachable within a few clicks, with descriptive anchor text. Orphan pages rarely rank.
- **Canonicals and hreflang**: consolidate duplicate content signals, and if you publish in multiple languages, make sure each version points to its alternates correctly.
- **Crawl budget basics**: clean XML sitemap, intentional robots.txt, no chains of redirects, and fast server response times.

On WordPress, most of this is handled by good themes and plugins — until a plugin conflict or theme update silently strips your schema. ## 5. Local SEO: Own Your Map Pack

Owning your map pack means a complete Google Business Profile, earning and answering reviews, genuinely local pages for each service area, and a name, address, and phone number that stay consistent everywhere. For businesses serving specific cities, the local pack often outranks the organic results below it — frequently the highest-visibility slot on the page. The work is unglamorous:

- Complete and actively maintain your Google Business Profile — categories, hours, photos, posts, and Q&A.
- Earn reviews steadily and respond to all of them, including the bad ones, professionally.
- Build dedicated pages for each location or service area with genuinely local content, not a find-and-replace of the city name.
- Keep your name, address, and phone number consistent across every directory and citation.

## 6. Content Depth Beats Keyword Density

Content depth means satisfying the query completely — answering the main question early, then covering the follow-ups a real reader has — instead of hitting a word count or keyword frequency. The "500 words at 2%" playbook is dead: pages win by matching intent and covering the topic, and how often the phrase appears is neither here nor there.

What that means in practice:

- Answer the main question early, then cover the follow-up questions a real reader would have.
- Match search intent: if the results page is full of comparison pages, don't publish a definition post.
- Use related terms and entities naturally — good writing covers synonyms without trying.
- Keep content updated. Refresh dates, examples, and screenshots; stale pages decay in rankings.
- Give the reader something they came for elsewhere: original screenshots, a worked example, a decision rule — the material a summarizer can attribute to you specifically.

Density still matters as a sanity check — if your target phrase appears zero times, or thirty times in 600 words, something is off. Run drafts through our [keyword density analyzer](/en/tools/keyword-density-analyzer/) to catch over- or under-optimization, but let reader value set the length.

## 7. AI-Assisted, Human-Edited Content Workflows

An AI-assisted workflow uses drafting tools for acceleration while a human owns accuracy, experience, and voice: outline and draft with AI, then edit, verify, and rewrite as the person responsible for what ships. The filter is now editorial rigor, not tooling — delegation without editing is what produces pages that read like everything else online.

1. **Research and outlining**: let AI cluster subtopics and surface questions to answer.
2. **First draft**: AI generates a structured draft from your outline and notes.
3. **Human editing pass — non-negotiable**: add first-hand experience, verify every claim, cut generic filler, and rewrite in your brand voice.
4. **Fact and link check**: AI invents statistics and citations with confidence. Every number gets verified or removed.
5. **Optimize and publish**: internal links, schema, and a readability pass.

Google has said plainly it doesn't penalize AI content — it penalizes unhelpful content. The risk isn't using AI; it's publishing what AI produces unedited, which reads like everything else on the internet and earns no links, no citations, and no rankings.

## 8. A Review Routine: Monthly, Quarterly, After Every Update

A review routine fixes who checks what and when: search performance monthly, declining content refreshed quarterly, and a technical sweep after every release that touches templates. Without a schedule, SEO feedback arrives as a traffic drop noticed late, and the cause is a stack of changes instead of one suspect. With a schedule, regressions surface while cheap to reverse.

- **Monthly**: open Search Console and compare impressions, clicks, and coverage against last month. Look first at pages that dropped — a sudden decline points at a change you made, while a slow drift points at content or competitors.
- **Quarterly**: refresh declining pages — new dates, current examples, replaced screenshots — and prune or merge pages that overlap the same query. Stale pages decay; maintained ones hold.
- **After every template, theme, or plugin update**: re-validate structured data, spot-check canonicals and titles on the affected templates, and confirm the sitemap still lists only canonical URLs.
- **After any migration or redesign**: full crawl, redirect-map verification, and coverage monitoring until the index settles.

Keep a one-page baseline document — index counts, impressions, and vital scores as of each quarterly audit — so every review is a comparison rather than a hunch. For the recurring checklist these reviews run against, use our [SEO checklist](/en/blog/seo-checklist-2026/).
