And the global numbers say most stores fail it — In 2026, Baymard Institute's meta-analysis of 50 cart-abandonment studies puts the average abandonment rate at 70.22%. Seven out of ten carts, gone.

## What does it take to win Tehran e-commerce in 2026?

Trust first, everything else second. Tehran shoppers hand over card details to sites they've often never bought from, so credibility engineering beats decoration: a verifiable Tehran address and phone number, transparent pricing with all fees visible before checkout, clear return policies, recognizable local payment options, and reviews from real buyers. Our [e-commerce development service](/en/services/ecommerce/) treats these as launch blockers, not nice-to-haves — a store without them is a funnel with a hole in it.

Speed is the second trust signal. Shoppers read slow pages as broken pages, and Tehran mobile networks punish heavy themes harshly. Compress images to modern formats, kill plugin bloat, and measure with field data — not just lab scores — because your customers' phones are the only benchmark that matters. Our [speed optimization guide](/en/blog/website-speed-optimization-pricing-guide-2026/) shows what the audit-to-fix process looks like and what it returns.

## What drives the cost of a Tehran online store?

Anyone quoting you a single number without asking about integrations is guessing. The honest cost drivers, in rough order of budget impact:

- **Product data**: 50 SKUs with clean photos versus 5,000 variable products with filters is a different project. Data entry and migration dwarf design hours on catalog-heavy builds.
- **Payment and logistics integrations**: local gateways, shipping rules, SMS notifications, and accounting sync each add integration and testing time.
- **Checkout UX work**: guest checkout, address handling for Tehran zones, error recovery — the unglamorous work that decides whether the 70.22% abandonment figure applies to you.
- **Custom logic**: real-time pricing, booking, multi-vendor flows. This is the line between WooCommerce-with-plugins and custom development.
- **SEO and content migration**: Persian keyword mapping, redirects from an old store, and schema. Skipping this is how redesigns lose half their traffic overnight.

## Why does checkout UX decide your revenue?

Because the losses are concentrated exactly where design can fix them. In 2026, Baymard's analysis of the non-browsing abandonment reasons found extra costs (shipping, tax, fees) too high at 39%, delivery too slow at 21%, forced account creation at 19%, card-trust worries at 19%, and long complicated checkouts at 18%. Every one of those is a design or copy decision — show totals early, offer guest checkout, surface delivery dates, and put trust signals at the payment step.

## Is your store mobile-first or mobile-last?

Be honest about where your customers are: on phones, on the move, comparing prices mid-decision. Mobile abandonment runs roughly ten or more points above desktop in every dataset — smaller screens make card entry, code application, and order review measurably harder, and forced-account or surprise-fee friction hurts more on a phone. Baymard's mobile research keeps finding the same basics missing: wrong keyboards on form fields, no guest path, hidden totals.

The Tehran-specific checklist: Persian RTL layout that doesn't break on small screens, numeric keyboards for card and phone fields, one-page or single-column checkout, sticky order total, and cash/delivery expectations stated upfront where relevant. Test on a mid-range Android over a throttled connection — that's your customer's device, not your MacBook.
